# Note on Channel Choice (SMS vs. Phone)

US research indicates that reaching people by phone alone is unreliable — most Americans don't answer calls from unknown numbers [1][2], and small businesses miss most of their inbound calls [3] — while SMS allows the recipient to respond when available and leaves a written record. This supports using SMS/email as the first touch, with calls as escalation. *Limitation: available sources cover consumers and general small businesses rather than B2B outreach to service centers, and the studies are several years old since no newer rigorous research was found; the pilot will measure our own per-channel response rates.*

## Sources (US only)

1. [Pew Research Center (2020) — Most Americans don't answer cellphone calls from unknown numbers](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2020/12/14/most-americans-dont-answer-cellphone-calls-from-unknown-numbers/) — national survey; consumers' personal phones only.
2. [TNS / Kantar (2022) — 75% of Americans will never answer calls from unknown numbers](https://tnsi.com/resource/com/tns-survey-75-americans-will-never-answer-calls-from-unknown-numbers-press-release/) — US survey of 1,000+ adults; industry-commissioned.
3. [411 Locals — SMBs don't answer 62% of phone calls](https://411locals.us/small-business-owners-dont-answer-62-of-phone-calls/) — vendor-run, 85 businesses; directional support only.
4. [Tabular — SMS marketing stats with open rates, engagement, and more](https://tabular.email/blog/sms-marketing-stats) — not trustworthy as evidence: a marketing roundup by an email-tools vendor that aggregates second-hand stats (e.g., the "295% higher response rate" figure) without methodology; included only as an example of the widely circulated claims this note does not rely on.
